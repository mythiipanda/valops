How it works

Player Elo
R′ = R + K(S − E)
E = 1 / (1 + 10(Rb − Ra)/400)

A player's rating moves toward their results. Beating a stronger team moves it more.

A team's rating is the mean of its five players, minus a penalty when the lineup is new.

Win probability
P(A beats B) = 1 / (1 + e−w·x)

The model weighs rating gaps and form: recent results, schedule strength, map pool, pistol rounds.

Two ratings feed it. A long-run Elo for true strength, and a faster form Elo that moves 1.5x on every result and never regresses in the offseason.

Simulation

We run 10,000 sims over the real groups and bracket.

Player scores
swing(round) = P(win | score + 1) − P(win | score)

Every round is worth its win-probability jump. An 11-11 round moves the needle far more than a 12-3 round, so clutch rounds pay more. A map's total swing is split across the winning five by kills, first kills, assists, and survival, weighted by role.

Space = (first kills + first deaths) / rounds

Space is engagement rate: how often a player takes the first duel of a round.

SWING-AR = (swing − replacement swing) / rounds × 100

Replacement level is the 20th percentile of the player's role, so a duelist is judged against duelists. Swing is opponent-adjusted first: credit scales as 1 + (opponent Elo − 1500) / 400, so beating an elite team pays more than farming a weak region.
