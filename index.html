How it works

The data

Every pro series, map by map and round by round: scores, kills, agents, economy. It all goes into one database, and every number on this page is built from games played before match day. Nothing here has seen the future.

Player ratings

Every player has a rating. Win and it climbs, lose and it falls — beating a strong team moves it more than beating a weak one. Ratings belong to players, not teams, so they survive roster moves. Each offseason they drift 30% back toward average, because last year fades and rosters change.

R′=R+K(S−E)E=11+10(Rb−Ra)/400R' = R + K(S - E) \qquad E = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_b - R_a)/400}}R′=R+K(S−E)E=1+10(Rb​−Ra​)/4001​

Your new rating is your old rating plus a step toward what actually happened. K is the step size, S is the result weighted by how much you drove it, E is how likely it looked beforehand.

When a team wins a series, the credit goes to whoever swung the rounds. Every kill is worth however much it moved the round's win probability — a 1v3 clutch pays more than a cleanup kill on a save round. And small samples get shrunk, not cut off: a player with n maps counts as n/(n+20) themselves and 20/(n+20) league average.

R^=nn+20R+20n+20Rˉ\hat{R} = \frac{n}{n+20}R + \frac{20}{n+20}\bar{R}R^=n+20n​R+n+2020​Rˉ

A team's rating is its five players averaged, minus a small penalty for a fresh lineup. The model keeps two trackers running side by side: a slow one for true strength, and a fast one that reacts 1.5x to every result and never regresses.

Round Swing

For every kill, ask: how much did this change the round's win probability? Add it up per 100 rounds, adjust for agents (some roles just find more action), and shrink small samples toward average. That's the number next to each player's name.

Swing=100R∑ΔP(round win)\mathrm{Swing} = \frac{100}{R}\sum \Delta P(\text{round win})Swing=R100​∑ΔP(round win)
What the model looks at

Every matchup becomes a list of A-minus-B gaps: rating, recent form, last-60-day stats (rating, ACS, KAST, first-kill differential), series win rate, strength of schedule, days of rest, agent coverage, pistol and retake rates. Thin samples get smoothed toward average, so a three-map hot streak can't break the math.

Turning gaps into a probability
P(A beats B)=11+e−w⋅xP(A \text{ beats } B) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-w \cdot x}}P(A beats B)=1+e−w⋅x1​

A plain logistic regression: one learned weight per gap, squashed into a number between 0 and 1. The heaviest weights sit on recent form, series win rate, and pistol rounds — but the features overlap, so no single weight tells the whole story. Both sides always add to exactly 1.

Does it work

Train on past years, test on the next one — the way you'd actually use it. The score is Brier: the average squared gap between the probability and what happened. 0 is perfect, 0.25 is flipping a coin. Lower wins.

Simulation

We run 10,000 sims over the real groups and bracket.

Player scores
swing(kill) = P(win round | after) − P(win round | before)

Every kill is worth the round-win probability it added. A first blood in a 1v1 is worth far more than a trade in a 5v2, so stat padding dies here. The probabilities come from 2023-2024 rounds, keyed on attackers alive, defenders alive, and whether the spike is planted. Credit is zero-sum: what the killer gains, the victim loses. Plants and defuses move the state but earn no direct credit.

Round Swing = Σ (swing − agent average) / rounds × 100

Each player is measured against the prior-year average for their agent, so a duelist is judged against duelists. The result is per 100 rounds, then shrunk toward zero for small samples. The full-year board weights recent splits more heavily and counts international games more than regional ones, the same way the model trusts recent form and big stages; stage filters show each split raw.
